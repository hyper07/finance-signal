Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “When information breaks the historical pattern: preliminary evidence from a deployed Bitcoin forecaster,” for publication in *Finance Research Letters*.

The paper reports a concise audit of next-session probabilistic forecasts from a deployed historical-analog system for BTC/USD and BITO. Average nominal-80% coverage is close to nominal, yet the distributions fail conditionally on large realized moves. Headline-count spikes are associated with larger standardized errors in both instruments, while directional degradation appears for BTC but does not replicate for BITO. Block-length and threshold checks support the narrow error-magnitude finding.

The result is preliminary but relevant to the journal's broad finance and forecasting readership: marginal calibration can conceal a reliability boundary precisely when information intensity or realized moves are large. We deliberately do not claim that headline counts cause returns or that the audit identifies investor psychology. Instead, the paper motivates preregistered research linking news content and expectedness to direct measures of investor response and prospectively testing forecast-reliability gates.
